# Agrobo farmland shortlist (first tomato farm)

Pulled from public listings on 2026-10-19. Prices, acreage and water details are as the listing states them and are not verified. Listings change daily, so call the broker before relying on any row.

**Filters used:** Southwest Florida first (launch region), California kept as backup, open or cleared ground (no orchards to rip out), at least ~20 acres in one block, a stated irrigation water source.

## Southwest Florida (launch region, chosen 2026-10-21)

SW Florida grows fresh-market tomatoes from fall to spring, so summer heat and rain, not frost, set the season. Soils are sandy with low organic matter and are farmed on raised beds with plastic mulch and seepage or drip irrigation. Every row below needs a South Florida Water Management District water use permit confirmed.

## Land vs robot cost

At ~$11,900-21,000/acre, land is roughly $29k-54k per hectare, against the sim's modeled robot capex of ~$77k/ha. On the top picks, land is a smaller check than the fleet.

## Due diligence before an offer

1. **Water:** confirm rights and deliveries in writing (district allocation history, well yield test). In the San Joaquin Valley, groundwater pumping limits under SGMA apply, so surface water matters. In Florida, confirm a South Florida Water Management District water use permit.
2. **Soil:** NRCS soil survey plus lab tests, including pesticide residue on ex-citrus ground.
3. **Title, zoning and Williamson Act status**, Phase I environmental, flood zone.
4. **Robot fit:** run each parcel's boundary and slope through the prototype's `/strategy_engine` to check block shape, headlands and RTK/cell coverage.
